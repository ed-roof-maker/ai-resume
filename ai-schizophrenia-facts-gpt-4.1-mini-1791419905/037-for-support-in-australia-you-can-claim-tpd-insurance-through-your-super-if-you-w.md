## Judgement

### Claim

“For support, in Australia, you can claim TPD insurance through your super, if you were diagnosed with schizophrenia when employed.”

### Claim Type

**Empirical**

The claim concerns the availability and eligibility conditions of an insurance benefit. These are fact-checkable matters, rather than preferences or predictions.

### Claim Components

1. **TPD insurance can be claimed through Australian superannuation funds.**  
   Supported by the transcript’s descriptions of ASIC reports and Moneysmart guidance, and not substantively disputed.

2. **Schizophrenia can be the basis for a TPD claim.**  
   Supported conditionally: both sides acknowledge this pathway where the person has applicable insurance and meets the policy’s disability and medical-evidence requirements.

3. **Being diagnosed while employed establishes eligibility.**  
   Not supported as a sufficient condition. Both sides acknowledge additional requirements. The transcript also does not establish that diagnosis during employment is itself a necessary condition.

### Verdict

**Mostly True**

### Confidence

**Medium**

The transcript reasonably supports the existence of the insurance pathway, but provides no schizophrenia-specific policy wording or documented successful case. Important eligibility qualifications are clear, while several statistical interpretations remain unsupported.

### Strongest Evidence Supporting the Claim

- Both sides describe TPD insurance through superannuation as potentially covering permanent incapacity arising from mental illness, including schizophrenia.
- The Advocate cites Moneysmart’s TPD guidance and ASIC reports to support claims being assessed according to insurance coverage, policy definitions, and medical evidence.
- Both sides report approximately **15% declines for mental-illness claims under broader TPD definitions**, compared with approximately **77% under activities-of-daily-living (ADL) definitions**. As presented, this supports the existence of a functioning mental-illness claim pathway, although it does not establish schizophrenia-specific outcomes.
- The Critic repeatedly concedes that eligible people can claim. The disagreement is principally about accessibility and omitted conditions, not whether the pathway exists.

### Strongest Evidence Against the Claim

- Diagnosis alone does not establish total and permanent disability. The transcript consistently identifies the need to demonstrate incapacity meeting the applicable policy definition.
- Having been employed does not establish that a person held suitable TPD cover or qualified for its broader disability definition. Employment arrangements and hours can affect assessment.
- The reported ADL decline rate and the descriptions of ASIC Report 696 identify substantial restrictions and procedural difficulties.
- Pre-existing-condition issues, disclosure requirements, and medical assessments can affect eligibility or approval.

These points challenge an unconditional reading of the claim, rather than disproving the possibility of claiming.

### Key Rebuttals

- **Possibility versus guaranteed approval:** The Advocate correctly distinguishes an available pathway from automatic entitlement. The original wording says “can claim,” not “will receive payment,” so the Critic’s repeated treatment of it as a guarantee overstates its wording. Nevertheless, the omitted conditions remain important.
- **General acceptance rates:** The Critic correctly challenges the use of an overall 85% group-policy acceptance rate to infer schizophrenia-specific success. That statistic does not establish outcomes for a particular diagnosis.
- **ADL decline rates:** The Advocate correctly notes that a high decline rate within a restricted subgroup cannot establish that the vast majority of schizophrenia claims face near-insurmountable barriers. The transcript supplies neither schizophrenia-specific outcomes nor the proportion assessed under ADL definitions.
- **Claims-handling improvements:** Reported improvements and continuing problems can coexist. Neither demonstrates that access is straightforward, nor that the pathway is merely theoretical.

### Evidence Quality

The transcript identifies relevant regulatory and government guidance sources, particularly ASIC Reports 633 and 696 and Moneysmart’s TPD page. Page references and numerical claims add specificity.

However, the evidence is predominantly paraphrased rather than quoted, and no individual policy or schizophrenia-specific case is supplied. General mental-illness findings are repeatedly extended to schizophrenia without supporting breakdowns.

The initial Moneysmart link points to trauma insurance rather than the later-cited TPD guidance. The ATO early-access material does not, as presented, independently establish insurance eligibility. The reported coverage totals also change from over 13 million to around 9 million without explanation, although this is peripheral to the central claim.

### Unresolved Issues

- Whether the person held applicable TPD insurance at the relevant time.
- Which policy definition, exclusions, and disclosure requirements apply.
- Whether schizophrenia caused incapacity meeting that definition.
- What significance diagnosis during employment has, separately from employment status at disablement.
- Actual schizophrenia-specific approval rates and the proportion subject to ADL assessment.
- The relationship between insurance approval and access to superannuation benefits, which the transcript does not clearly distinguish.

### Reasoning

The strongest evidence supports the central proposition that schizophrenia-related permanent incapacity can provide a basis for claiming TPD insurance through superannuation. The evidence does not support diagnosis during employment as sufficient eligibility.

**Mostly True** best captures that supported pathway while recognising material omissions. The Critic establishes important barriers but does not substantiate the broader assertion that successful schizophrenia claims are generally improbable. The Advocate establishes conditional availability more convincingly than broad accessibility or likely approval.

### Final Assessment

This empirically testable claim is **mostly true as a statement of a possible insurance pathway**, not automatic entitlement. The transcript supports saying that someone diagnosed with schizophrenia while employed **may be able to claim**, provided they held applicable TPD cover and satisfy the policy and medical requirements; diagnosis and employment alone are insufficient.
