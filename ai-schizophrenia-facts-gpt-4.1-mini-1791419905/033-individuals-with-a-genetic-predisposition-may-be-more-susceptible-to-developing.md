**Causal**

### Verdict

**Insufficient Evidence**

### Confidence

**Medium**

The transcript contains relevant, suggestive evidence, but the central claim requires connecting genetic vulnerability, cannabis exposure, and subsequent schizophrenia development. That connection is supported indirectly rather than clearly demonstrated, and important source interpretations remain disputed.

- **Polygenic risk score findings:** The Advocate describes a UK Biobank study involving over 100,000 participants in which schizophrenia genetic risk modified the association between cannabis use and psychotic experiences. The transcript provides more evidence limiting the conclusion than directly disproving it:

- **Different outcomes:** Psychotic experiences and cannabis-induced psychosis are not equivalent to developing schizophrenia. Evidence about the former cannot automatically establish the latter.
- **Mixed genetic findings:** The Critic cites inconsistent replication of candidate-gene associations and a cohort report finding no significant familial-predisposition difference between cannabis users who developed psychosis and those who did not. Details are insufficient to establish how strongly this challenges the broader claim.

No clearly described study in the transcript decisively rules out the qualified claim.

The transcript identifies research articles, study designs, an approximate sample size, and numerical MR estimates. These are more informative than unsupported assertions alone.
