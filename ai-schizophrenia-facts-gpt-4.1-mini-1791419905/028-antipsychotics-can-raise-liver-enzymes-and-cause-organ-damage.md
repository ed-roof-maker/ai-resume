## [1] ADVOCATE

Antipsychotics can indeed raise liver enzymes and cause organ damage, and this is supported by medical research and clinical evidence.

1. Liver Enzyme Elevation: Many antipsychotic drugs, especially some first-generation antipsychotics like chlorpromazine and haloperidol, have been documented to cause elevated serum liver enzymes. Elevated liver enzymes are a marker of liver cell injury or stress. For example, clozapine, a second-generation antipsychotic, is associated with an asymptomatic rise in liver enzymes in up to 60% of patients, with 15-30% experiencing a twofold or threefold elevation. This elevation is often transient but indicates liver involvement (source: PMC article on antipsychotic-induced hepatotoxicity, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7781826).

2. Hepatotoxicity and Liver Damage: Beyond enzyme elevation, some antipsychotics have been linked to more severe liver damage, including drug-induced liver injury (DILI). Cases of severe liver damage and even fulminant liver failure have been reported with clozapine use. Other antipsychotics like chlorpromazine have also been associated with severe liver damage (source: same PMC article).

3. Mechanism: Antipsychotics are metabolized primarily in the liver by cytochrome P450 enzymes. This metabolism can sometimes lead to toxic intermediates or immune-mediated liver injury, causing hepatocellular damage or cholestatic lesions.

4. Risk Variation: While atypical antipsychotics generally have a lower risk of severe liver toxicity, asymptomatic increases in liver enzymes are still common. Some antipsychotics like chlorpromazine, clozapine, and olanzapine pose higher risks, while others like haloperidol have a lower but still present risk (source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36180017/).

5. Clinical Recommendations: Due to these risks, liver function tests are recommended during antipsychotic treatment, especially with higher-risk drugs. If liver enzymes rise significantly (e.g., more than three times the upper limit of normal), discontinuation or dose adjustment is advised to prevent further liver damage (source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5292604).

In summary, the evidence clearly shows that antipsychotics can raise liver enzymes and, in some cases, cause liver damage. This risk varies by drug type and patient factors but is significant enough to warrant monitoring and caution in clinical use.
