This cycle shows stigma as a root cause that intensifies other barriers like unemployment and social withdrawal, rather than being a minor or secondary factor.

3. **Stigma as a Structural Barrier in Healthcare**  
WHO’s mental health action plan identifies stigma within healthcare systems as a structural barrier affecting provider attitudes, treatment quality, and patient trust, worsening access and outcomes even where services exist (https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/schizophrenia). This structural stigma compounds systemic resource limitations, making stigma reduction essential alongside improving healthcare infrastructure.

4. **Intrinsic Illness Factors and Stigma Are Intertwined**  
While symptoms and cognitive impairments inherent to schizophrenia affect social functioning, stigma independently adds external social barriers that worsen isolation and reduce opportunities for social engagement and support. The critic’s framing of intrinsic factors as independent downplays how stigma interacts with and exacerbates these impairments.

5. **Complexity Does Not Negate Stigma’s Central Role**  
The critic rightly notes the multifactorial nature of challenges faced by people with schizophrenia, but complexity does not diminish stigma’s central and causal role. Instead, stigma is a foundational force that shapes and intensifies other barriers, making it a critical target for intervention to improve social interactions and access to care.

In summary, the WHO’s authoritative position supports the claim that stigma and misunderstanding surrounding schizophrenia are foundational, causal factors significantly affecting social exclusion and access to care. While other factors contribute, stigma is a root cause that amplifies and sustains these challenges. Addressing stigma is indispensable for breaking the cycle of discrimination and improving outcomes for people with schizophrenia.
