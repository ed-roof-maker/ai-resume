## [1] ADVOCATE

The government should ensure multinational businesses operating in Australia pay their fair share of taxes without increasing the GST or reintroducing death duties for several strong reasons:

1. Multinational businesses already contribute a significant portion of Australia's corporate tax revenue. Large public and multinational businesses, especially those with turnovers over $250 million, pay about 67% of company tax in Australia. This shows their critical role in funding public services without needing to raise GST or reintroduce death duties.

2. The Australian Taxation Office (ATO) has effective measures in place to ensure compliance and address tax avoidance by multinationals. Since 2016, the ATO's Tax Avoidance Taskforce has raised $29 billion in liabilities from public and multinational businesses by scrutinizing aggressive tax arrangements and ensuring these companies pay what they owe. This demonstrates that the government can secure fair tax contributions through enforcement rather than broad tax increases.

3. Laws like the Multinational Anti-Avoidance Law (MAAL) and Diverted Profits Tax (DPT) specifically target profit shifting and artificial arrangements used by multinationals to avoid tax. These laws help maintain a strong domestic tax regime that ensures multinationals pay tax on profits earned in Australia, protecting the tax base without burdening ordinary consumers through GST hikes.

4. Increasing GST or reintroducing death duties would disproportionately affect ordinary Australians. GST is a consumption tax paid by all consumers, including low- and middle-income households, making it regressive. Death duties can be seen as punitive and politically unpopular. Ensuring multinationals pay their fair share through targeted compliance is a fairer and more efficient approach.

In summary, the government can and should focus on enforcing existing tax laws and strengthening compliance measures to ensure multinationals pay their fair share. This approach protects revenue without increasing GST or reintroducing death duties, which would unfairly impact everyday Australians.
