## [1] ADVOCATE

The strongest argument for reforming the Australian Family Law system to prioritize fair child support payments that directly benefit children rather than ex-partners is centered on ensuring that financial support truly serves the child's best interests and well-being.

Currently, child support payments are intended to contribute to the costs of raising children after separation, but the system can sometimes result in funds being used in ways that do not directly benefit the child. Reforming the system to focus explicitly on the child's needs would ensure that payments are used for essentials such as education, health, nutrition, and extracurricular activities, rather than being absorbed into the general expenses of the ex-partner receiving the payments.

The Australian Government's child support framework already recognizes different care arrangements and adjusts payments accordingly, such as reducing payments when the paying parent provides regular or shared care (e.g., 14% to 65% care reduces payments) and exempting payments when a parent has primary care (65% or more) (source: humanservices.gov.au). This shows an existing focus on fairness and care involvement, but there is room to strengthen the system to guarantee that payments are transparently and exclusively benefiting the child.

Prioritizing fair child support payments would:
1. Enhance the child's quality of life by ensuring funds are spent on their direct needs.
2. Reduce conflicts and disputes between ex-partners over money, focusing attention on the child's welfare.
3. Encourage responsible parenting by linking financial support more clearly to the child's care and development.
4. Align with the Family Law Act's principle that the child's best interests are paramount in parenting decisions.

In summary, reforming the Family Law system to prioritize child support payments that directly benefit children ensures that financial resources serve their intended purpose—supporting the child's upbringing and well-being—rather than becoming a source of contention or indirect support for ex-partners.
