## [1] ADVOCATE

The Australian government should implement policies to achieve timber self-sufficiency by expanding domestic forestry plantations and reducing reliance on timber imports for several strong reasons:

1. Economic Benefits and Job Creation: Expanding domestic forestry plantations supports a significant sector of the Australian economy. Forestry industries contribute about $24 billion annually and directly support around 80,000 jobs, with another 100,000 jobs indirectly linked to the sector. Increasing plantation forestry would create more employment opportunities, particularly in rural and regional areas facing population decline and economic challenges. This would strengthen local economies and reduce unemployment (source: onenation.org.au).

2. Housing and Construction Supply Security: Australia currently relies heavily on timber imports, which can cause supply chain disruptions, increased costs, and delays in housing and construction projects. Achieving timber self-sufficiency would stabilize timber supply, helping to lower construction costs and support the housing market, which is critical given Australia's ongoing housing affordability issues (source: onenation.org.au).

3. Environmental and Carbon Benefits: Plantation forests absorb carbon dioxide, helping to mitigate climate change. Australian forestry plantations currently store about 258 million tonnes of carbon. Expanding plantations would increase carbon sequestration, contributing positively to Australia's climate goals while providing sustainable timber resources (source: onenation.org.au).

4. Existing Capacity and Government Support: Australia already produces about two-thirds of its domestic timber from plantations, showing strong existing capacity. Government initiatives like the National Forest Policy Statement and the "Plantations for Australia: The 2020 Vision" program aim to expand commercial plantations to meet future demand, indicating policy alignment and feasibility for further expansion (source: forestry.org.au, qut.edu.au).

In summary, expanding domestic forestry plantations to achieve timber self-sufficiency would boost the economy, secure housing and construction supply, provide environmental benefits, and build on existing government-supported frameworks. This makes it a sound policy direction for Australia.
