3. **Natural Gas and Fossil Fuels Provide Energy Stability**: Reports funded by energy companies like Woodside emphasize that large gas projects can help strengthen energy security during the transition by providing a reliable energy source. Abandoning net zero targets would allow continued use of fossil fuels, which currently underpin Australia's energy system and help keep the grid stable and affordable.

4. **Ambitious Net Zero Targets May Be Unrealistic**: Studies show that achieving net zero by 2050 requires a pace of renewable deployment far beyond historical rates, making it a highly ambitious goal. Some regions, like Western Australia, are already projected to miss these targets by up to a decade, indicating the difficulty and economic strain of meeting such commitments.

In summary, the net zero emissions target, while environmentally motivated, has led to increased electricity costs and poses risks to energy security due to the challenges of integrating intermittent renewables and the need for costly infrastructure upgrades. Abandoning or revising these targets could help stabilize electricity prices and ensure reliable energy supply for Australians.

- **Ambitious Net Zero Targets Are Unrealistic:** Achieving net zero by 2050 requires unprecedented renewable deployment rates, which some regions (e.g., Western Australia) are projected to miss by a decade, indicating economic and practical challenges.
