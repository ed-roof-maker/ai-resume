Limiting leasehold tenure to 25 years ensures that farmland remains accessible to Australian farmers in the long term, preventing indefinite control by foreign entities and encouraging local stewardship of the land.

3. National Control Over Strategic Assets: Farmland is a strategic national asset linked to environmental sustainability, rural economies, and cultural heritage. Allowing foreign investors unrestricted freehold ownership risks losing control over these assets. Leasehold limits provide a balance by allowing foreign investment but with clear time boundaries that protect Australia's sovereignty and ability to regulate land use in the public interest.

In summary, banning further freehold sales to foreign investors and limiting leasehold tenure to 25 years safeguards Australia's food security, supports local farmers, and maintains national control over vital agricultural land. This policy is a prudent measure to protect Australia's long-term agricultural future and rural communities.
