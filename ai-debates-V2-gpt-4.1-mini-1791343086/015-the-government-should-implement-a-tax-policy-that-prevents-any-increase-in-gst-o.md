1. Protecting Low- and Middle-Income Households: Increasing the Goods and Services Tax (GST) disproportionately affects low- and middle-income households because GST is a consumption tax applied uniformly to most goods and services. These households spend a larger proportion of their income on consumption, so raising GST would increase their financial burden and exacerbate inequality. Preventing GST increases helps maintain affordability and economic fairness.

2. Avoiding the Negative Impact of Death Duties: Death duties (inheritance taxes) can discourage wealth transfer within families and create administrative burdens. Opposing their reintroduction protects family wealth and avoids complexities that may not generate significant revenue relative to the social and economic costs.

3. Ensuring Multinational Corporations Pay Their Fair Share: Multinational corporations often use complex tax structures to minimize their tax liabilities, depriving Australia of significant revenue. Ensuring these corporations pay their fair share promotes tax fairness, funds essential public services, and supports economic sovereignty. This can be achieved through targeted policies and international cooperation to close loopholes.

In summary, this tax policy balances protecting vulnerable households, preserving family wealth, and promoting corporate tax fairness, which together support social equity and sustainable government revenue.
