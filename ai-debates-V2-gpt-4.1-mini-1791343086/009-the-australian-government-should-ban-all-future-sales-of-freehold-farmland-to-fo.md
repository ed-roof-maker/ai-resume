The Australian government should ban all future sales of freehold farmland to foreign investors and restrict leasehold farmland sales to a maximum tenure of 25 years for several compelling reasons centered on national sovereignty, food security, and economic stability.

1. National Sovereignty and Control: Farmland is a critical national asset. Allowing foreign investors to own freehold farmland means relinquishing long-term control over vital resources. This can lead to decisions about land use and production that prioritize foreign interests over Australia's national interests. By banning freehold sales and limiting leasehold terms, Australia retains sovereignty over its agricultural land, ensuring it is managed in ways that benefit the country and its citizens.

2. Food Security: Agriculture is fundamental to Australia's food supply. Foreign ownership of farmland can jeopardize food security if land is used primarily for export-oriented production or speculative purposes rather than sustainable food production for domestic needs. Restricting foreign ownership and lease terms helps ensure farmland remains dedicated to producing food for Australians, reducing vulnerability to global market fluctuations and geopolitical tensions.

3. Economic Stability and Local Benefit: When foreign investors own farmland outright, profits may be repatriated overseas, limiting economic benefits to local communities. Shorter lease terms encourage more local involvement and oversight, fostering investment that supports regional economies and employment. This approach balances attracting foreign capital with protecting local economic interests.

4. Environmental Stewardship: Long-term foreign ownership may prioritize short-term gains over sustainable land management. Limiting lease terms encourages accountability and responsible stewardship aligned with Australia's environmental standards and long-term agricultural viability.

In summary, banning freehold sales and restricting leasehold tenure to 25 years protects Australia's sovereignty, food security, economic interests, and environmental sustainability. It ensures farmland remains a national asset managed primarily for the benefit of Australians rather than foreign investors.
