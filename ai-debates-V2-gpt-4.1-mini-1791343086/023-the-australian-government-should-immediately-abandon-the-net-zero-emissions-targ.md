## [1] ADVOCATE

The Australian government should immediately abandon the ‘net zero’ emissions target to protect energy affordability, job security, and industrial competitiveness for several compelling reasons:

1. Energy Affordability: The push toward net zero often involves rapid transitions to renewable energy sources and the phasing out of fossil fuels. This transition can lead to higher energy costs due to the current higher costs of renewable infrastructure, grid instability, and reliance on intermittent energy sources. Higher energy prices disproportionately affect low- and middle-income households and can increase the cost of living significantly.

2. Job Security: Many Australian jobs are tied to fossil fuel industries such as coal mining, natural gas extraction, and related manufacturing. Abruptly abandoning these industries in pursuit of net zero risks massive job losses, particularly in regional areas where alternative employment opportunities are limited. Protecting these jobs is crucial for social stability and economic well-being.

3. Industrial Competitiveness: Australian industries, especially energy-intensive sectors like manufacturing and mining, face higher production costs if energy prices rise due to net zero policies. This can make Australian products less competitive internationally, leading to reduced exports and economic decline. Maintaining affordable and reliable energy is essential to keep these industries viable.

4. Technological and Economic Realities: Achieving net zero emissions is technologically challenging and economically costly in the short to medium term. Current renewable technologies and storage solutions are not yet capable of fully replacing fossil fuels without significant reliability and cost issues. Rushing to net zero could undermine economic growth and energy security.

In summary, abandoning the net zero target would prioritize affordable energy, protect jobs in critical sectors, and maintain Australia's industrial competitiveness, ensuring a stable and prosperous economy while allowing more time for technological advancements to make a sustainable transition feasible and economically viable.

# Debate Summary: Should the Australian Government Immediately Abandon the ‘Net Zero’ Emissions Target?

---

## 1. Claim

The Australian government should immediately abandon the ‘net zero’ emissions target to protect energy affordability, job security, and industrial competitiveness.

---

## 2. Advocate's Main Arguments

- **Energy Affordability:** The transition to net zero involves rapid shifts to renewable energy and phasing out fossil fuels, which currently leads to higher energy costs due to expensive renewable infrastructure, grid instability, and intermittency. This disproportionately impacts low- and middle-income households, increasing living costs.

- **Job Security:** Many Australian jobs depend on fossil fuel industries (coal mining, natural gas, manufacturing). Abruptly ending these industries risks massive job losses, especially in regional areas with limited alternative employment. Protecting these jobs is vital for social and economic stability.

- **Industrial Competitiveness:** Energy-intensive sectors face rising production costs from net zero policies, making Australian products less competitive internationally. Maintaining affordable, reliable energy is essential to keep these industries viable.

- **Technological and Economic Realities:** Current renewable technologies and storage solutions are not yet capable of fully replacing fossil fuels without reliability and cost issues. Achieving net zero is technologically challenging and economically costly in the short to medium term. Rushing could undermine economic growth and energy security.

- **Pragmatic Approach:** Abandoning net zero prioritizes affordability, job protection, and competitiveness, allowing more time for technological advancements and a sustainable, economically viable transition.

---

## 3. Critic's Main Arguments

- **Energy Affordability:** Rising energy prices are influenced by multiple factors beyond net zero policies, including global fuel price volatility and aging fossil fuel infrastructure. Renewables are becoming cost-competitive, stabilizing the grid as coal plants retire, and creating new revenue streams, especially in regional areas. Abandoning net zero risks locking Australia into costly, outdated fossil fuel infrastructure.

- **Job Security:** While fossil fuel jobs are at risk, renewable energy sectors are growing and creating new jobs, including in regional communities. Transition can be managed with retraining and support. Abandoning net zero may delay inevitable industry changes and prolong economic uncertainty.

- **Industrial Competitiveness:** Many countries are pursuing emissions reductions; global markets increasingly factor carbon costs. Australian industries that fail to innovate risk carbon tariffs and losing market access. Embracing cleaner technologies can future-proof and enhance competitiveness.

- **Technological and Economic Realities:** The transition to net zero is irreversible despite short-term costs. Delaying action will likely increase future costs due to climate impacts and abrupt adjustments. Managing the transition effectively is preferable to abandonment.

- **Long-Term Perspective:** Abandoning net zero risks missing economic opportunities in renewables, increasing long-term costs and risks, and locking Australia into an unsustainable energy future.

---

## 4. Key Rebuttals

- **Advocate Rebuttal to Critic:**
  - The critic underestimates the immediate economic pain caused by net zero policies, with energy prices having risen about 40% since commitment, disproportionately affecting vulnerable households.

  - Energy bills are unlikely to fall for at least a decade, so abandoning net zero allows a more affordable, measured transition.

- **Critic Rebuttal to Advocate:**
  - The advocate’s attribution of rising energy costs solely to net zero is flawed; multiple factors contribute.
  - Renewable energy is increasingly reliable and cost-effective, creating new jobs and revenue.
  - Abandoning net zero ignores global trends and risks future economic disadvantages.
  - The transition is inevitable and irreversible; delaying it increases long-term costs and risks.
