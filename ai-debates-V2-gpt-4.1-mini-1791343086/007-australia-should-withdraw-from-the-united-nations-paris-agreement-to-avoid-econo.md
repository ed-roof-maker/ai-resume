## 1. Claim

Australia should withdraw from the United Nations Paris Agreement to avoid economic harm caused by aggressive carbon emission cuts.

---

## 2. Advocate's Main Arguments

- **Economic Impact on Key Industries:** Australia's economy heavily depends on fossil fuel sectors (coal, natural gas, exports). Aggressive emission cuts threaten these industries with higher costs, reduced competitiveness, and job losses, especially in regional areas.

- **Energy Costs and Competitiveness:** Emission reduction targets increase energy costs due to investments in renewables and carbon pricing, raising production expenses and risking economic contraction.

- **Sovereignty and Flexibility:** Withdrawal would free Australia from international scrutiny and pressure, allowing tailored climate policies that balance economic interests and sustainable development at a manageable pace.

- **Limited Global Impact:** Australia's emissions are a small fraction of global totals; thus, its aggressive cuts have limited climate benefits but significant domestic economic costs.

- **Transition Realities:** The renewable transition is neither immediate nor cost-free. Withdrawal allows managing the transition aligned with Australia's economic realities, avoiding premature shocks.

- **Renewable Energy Challenges:** Australia's unique energy system requires costly infrastructure upgrades for renewables; withdrawal avoids binding targets that might force rushed, expensive transitions.

- **Economic Stability and Climate Adaptation:** Aggressive emission cuts harming the economy could reduce Australia's capacity to invest in climate adaptation and resilience.

- **National Interest over Global Pressure:** Protecting core industries and economic stability is paramount; withdrawal preserves sovereignty and allows maintaining international relations through other channels.

---

## 3. Critic's Main Arguments

- **Adaptation and Economic Opportunity:** Fossil fuel industries must adapt; global energy markets are shifting toward renewables. Withdrawal ignores emerging green sector opportunities and risks long-term economic decline.

- **Renewable Energy Cost Decline:** Renewable technologies have rapidly decreased in cost, potentially lowering energy prices and enhancing Australia's competitiveness. Paris Agreement participation encourages innovation and access to green finance.

- **Flexibility within Paris:** Australia's nationally determined contributions (NDCs) allow policy flexibility. Withdrawal risks diplomatic isolation and harms trade partnerships increasingly tied to climate commitments.

- **Collective Global Action:** Despite Australia's small emissions share, collective efforts are essential. Withdrawal could encourage other countries to backslide, worsening climate risks that ultimately harm Australia's economy.

- **Economic Risks of Fossil Fuel Dependence:** Global demand for fossil fuels is declining; continued reliance risks stranded assets and greater future economic harm.

- **Climate-Driven Economic Costs:** Australia already faces rising costs from climate-related disasters (floods, fires, storms) that damage infrastructure and increase insurance premiums.

- **Global Investment and Cooperation:** Staying in the Paris Agreement attracts global capital and innovation critical for economic adaptation and transition.

- **Sovereignty through Engagement:** Australia's influence and economic interests are better served by active participation in global climate frameworks than by withdrawal.
