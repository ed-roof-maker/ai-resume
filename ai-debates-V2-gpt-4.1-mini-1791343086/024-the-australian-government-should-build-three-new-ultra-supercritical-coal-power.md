## [1] ADVOCATE

The Australian government should build three new ultra-supercritical coal power plants to enhance energy security and support regional economies for several key reasons:

1. **Energy Security and Reliability**: Ultra-supercritical coal power plants are highly efficient and capable of providing a stable, continuous supply of electricity. Unlike intermittent renewable sources such as solar and wind, coal plants can operate 24/7 regardless of weather conditions. This reliability is crucial for preventing blackouts and ensuring that homes, businesses, and essential services have uninterrupted power.

2. **Economic Support for Regional Communities**: Coal power plants are often located in regional areas where coal mining and related industries provide significant employment. Building new plants would create jobs during construction and operation, supporting local economies that may be struggling due to the decline of traditional industries. This can help sustain communities and prevent economic decline in these regions.

3. **Technological Advancement and Efficiency**: Ultra-supercritical technology represents a significant improvement over older coal plants, operating at higher temperatures and pressures to achieve greater efficiency and lower emissions per unit of electricity generated. This means that while coal is still used, its environmental impact is reduced compared to older plants.

4. **Transition Strategy**: While renewable energy is growing, the transition to a fully renewable grid will take time. New ultra-supercritical coal plants can serve as a bridge, providing reliable power as renewable infrastructure and storage technologies mature. This balanced approach avoids energy shortages and price spikes.

In summary, building three new ultra-supercritical coal power plants would enhance Australia's energy security by providing reliable and efficient power, support regional economies through job creation, and serve as a pragmatic transition step toward a more sustainable energy future.
