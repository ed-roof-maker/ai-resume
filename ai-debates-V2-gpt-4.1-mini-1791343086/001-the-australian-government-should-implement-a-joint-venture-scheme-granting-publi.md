## [1] ADVOCATE

The Australian government should implement a joint venture scheme granting public ownership of up to 30% in oil and gas projects and replace the Petroleum Resource Rent Tax (PRRT) with a simple royalty on all extracted resources for several compelling reasons:

1. **Ensuring Fair Public Benefit from Natural Resources**  
Australia's oil and gas resources are finite and belong to the public. Granting the government up to 30% ownership in these projects ensures that the public directly benefits from the profits generated, rather than relying solely on complex tax arrangements. This ownership stake would provide a steady and transparent revenue stream that reflects the true value of the resources extracted.

2. **Simplifying and Increasing Revenue through a Royalty System**  
The current PRRT is a complicated tax system that has been criticized for allowing companies to minimize payments through deductions and accounting strategies, resulting in lower-than-expected government revenue. Replacing it with a simple royalty on all extracted resources would create a more straightforward, transparent, and reliable revenue mechanism. Royalties are easier to administer and less prone to avoidance, ensuring consistent income for public services and infrastructure.

3. **Promoting Accountability and Sustainable Resource Management**  
With a direct ownership stake, the government would have a stronger role in overseeing oil and gas projects, encouraging responsible environmental practices and sustainable resource management. This involvement can help balance economic benefits with environmental protection, aligning industry operations with public interest.

4. **Reducing Dependence on Volatile Market Conditions**  
A royalty system provides revenue based on production volume rather than profits, which can fluctuate due to market prices and company accounting. This stability is crucial for government budgeting and long-term planning, especially in sectors as volatile as oil and gas.

In summary, a joint venture scheme combined with a simple royalty system would enhance public benefit, increase government revenue transparency and stability, and promote responsible resource management. This approach aligns with principles of fairness and good governance, ensuring Australia's natural wealth supports the broader community effectively.
